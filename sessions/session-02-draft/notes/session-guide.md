## Before starting

1. Use your **7i for every measured shot**, with the device set to 7i. Keep the same target, hitting position, ball type and simulator conditions throughout.
2. Use your usual grip, stance and ball position. No stagger, deliberate hip shift or headcover.
3. Take a few easy no-ball warm-up swings with clear space around you. No stretch or turn should be forced.
4. Choose a comfortable normal swing length and effort that you can repeat—not a hip-to-hip drill swing and not a maximum-speed swing. Do not lengthen the swing just to chase distance.
5. Plan for **20 recorded shots**, with breaks. This is a planned amount, not an obligation to finish if tired or uncomfortable.

## Block A — Record your starting swing

**Purpose of this drill is to record your comfortable normal swing before adding a cue.**

**Setup:** Normal 7i address, one target, chosen comfortable swing length and effort.

**What to do:** Look at the target, use your usual routine and hit five shots. Do not consciously arrange hips, chest and arms in sequence. Keep every recorded shot, including mishits.

**Feel:** Your own normal swing. There is nothing to correct yet.

**Repetitions:** Five shots.

**Troubleshooting:** Do not respond to a short shot by swinging harder. If you make a substantial setup or device change, note it; do not quietly treat the shots as comparable.

## Blocks B1 and B2 — Swing through, rather than manage body parts

**Purpose of this drill is to test whether one continuous swing-through intention improves repeatable delivery without adding mechanical complexity.**

**Setup:** Exactly the same stance, ball position, target, intended swing length and effort as Block A. There is no obstacle or special foot position.

**What to do:**

1. Before each set of five, make one easy no-ball rehearsal at your chosen swing length. Let the club move back and through continuously.
2. Address the ball normally. Use just one cue: **“Swing through to my finish.”**
3. Let the club continue beyond the ball as your arms and body move through naturally. Do not deliberately shove the hips, hold the arms back, reach toward the target or try to swing more inside-out.
4. Let the finish happen. Do not manufacture a high finish, extra rotation or a held pose.
5. Hit five shots, rest briefly, then repeat the same five-shot block.

**Feel:** The ball is on the way through—not the place where the swing stops. This is an intention, not a command to accelerate harder.

**Repetitions:** Two sets of five shots; ten in total.

**Troubleshooting:** If the cue makes you tense, swing harder or force a finish, drop it and return to your normal routine. Record that change afterwards. Do not add another mechanical cue to rescue the experiment. Stop if uncomfortable.

## Block C — Take the cue away

**Purpose of this drill is to see whether any useful change carries into your normal routine without consciously doing the drill.**

**Setup:** Same normal stance, target and intended swing length/effort.

**What to do:** Hit five shots with your usual target-based routine. No rehearsal devoted to the cue and no technical instruction during the swing.

**Feel:** Play the shot rather than perform an exercise.

**Repetitions:** Five shots.

**Troubleshooting:** Do not bring back the cue to chase a good final number. Keep mishits and note fatigue or any change in effort.

## How we will review it

Compare A, B1, B2 and C using carry/total, ball-speed variation, available strike photographs and path/face readings together. Keep sample sizes visible and all shots included. Club-speed and smash-factor zeros in Session 01 were unavailable data, not evidence of low efficiency.

A cue is worth revisiting if there is a useful pattern across both cue blocks, delivery/strike becomes more repeatable without extra tension, and some benefit remains in C. Five-shot blocks are exploratory; they do not establish a durable improvement or a causal mechanism. Fatigue, order and small samples can affect the comparison.

If there is no clear benefit, we will not force the cue or conclude that your sequence is wrong. The next decision should follow the observed strike and delivery evidence, with video if available.

**Leave out this time:** Headcover drills, staggered stance, deliberate pressure/hip commands, driver practice and maximum-distance attempts. Optional play afterwards is separate from these practice blocks; record it separately and only review relevant 7i shots.
