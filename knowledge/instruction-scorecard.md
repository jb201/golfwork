# Golf swing instruction scorecard

## Purpose

This scorecard lets you rank each swing concept, drill, or online instruction against your actual goal: improve sequencing, reduce over-the-top motion, and add distance with the 7-iron before the driver.

Each source gets a score from 0 to 5 in each category. Total score helps you decide what deserves repetition and what should be discarded.

## Scoring rubric

### 1) Sequencing improvement
How much does it improve the order from lower body to torso to arms?

- 0 = no clear sequencing benefit
- 1 = minor or confusing effect
- 2 = somewhat helpful but inconsistent
- 3 = clear but still requires repetition
- 4 = strong repeated benefit
- 5 = clearly improves sequence and feels natural

### 2) Over-the-top reduction
How much does it reduce the sense of coming over the top or swinging from the arms?

- 0 = worsens or does not address it
- 1 = little impact
- 2 = some difference but inconsistent
- 3 = noticeable improvement on some reps
- 4 = strong reduction in the pattern
- 5 = consistent improvement and clear path change

### 3) Weight shift and pressure transfer
Does it improve the pressure shift and loading of the trail side before release?

- 0 = not addressed
- 1 = slight clue only
- 2 = some progress but not repeatable
- 3 = clear improvement with reps
- 4 = strong and repeatable pressure shift
- 5 = very clear and natural pressure transfer

### 4) Strike quality and contact
Does it improve contact, center-face contact, or consistent ball strike?

- 0 = worse contact
- 1 = inconsistent
- 2 = minor improvement
- 3 = some improvement
- 4 = strong improvement with quality strike
- 5 = consistently better contact and compressions

### 5) Distance effect
Does it improve carry or total distance without creating tension or a forced swing?

- 0 = no gain or worse distance
- 1 = minor and inconsistent
- 2 = some gain but not repeatable
- 3 = reliable gain on quality reps
- 4 = strong gain with control
- 5 = clear improvement in 7-iron distance pattern and driver potential

### 6) Practicality and repeatability
Can it be used regularly in a realistic practice session without overcomplicating the swing?

- 0 = too complex
- 1 = difficult to repeat
- 2 = usable but clunky
- 3 = manageable practical cue
- 4 = easy to rehearse and repeat
- 5 = simple, repeatable, and valuable in practice

## Weekly use

- Score each concept after 1-3 practice sessions.
- Keep only the sources with a total of 20+ and no category below 3 in the most important areas.
- Any concept scoring under 15 should be deprioritized or tested only once.
- If a drill or cue helps with the pattern but not distance, keep it as a support drill only.

## Priority score thresholds

- 25-30: strong candidate for repetition
- 20-24: useful but needs more validation
- 15-19: interesting but not yet a core fix
- 0-14: likely not worth the time for this phase

## The goal of the scorecard

The scorecard is a simple way to turn the swing process into a game:

- test ideas
- score them
- keep the winners
- remove the noise

This keeps the project anchored on the real objective: a more natural, better sequenced swing that produces more distance without forcing the move.
